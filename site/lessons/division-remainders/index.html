Keep the quotient and the remainder

Choose signed or unsigned division and inspect the remainder left by the same operation.

An average divides a total by a count. The number being divided is the dividend; the number you divide by is the divisor. The whole-number result is the quotient. The remainder is what remains after subtracting quotient times divisor from the dividend.

div t2, t0, t1 divides the signed value in t0 by the signed value in t1. It discards the fractional part towards zero. rem t3, t0, t1 supplies the corresponding remainder. Keep both original inputs until both instructions have read them.

divu and remu instead read both inputs as unsigned. The example uses signed instructions on a negative dividend. Predict the quotient in t2 after the complete fragment, then inspect the remainder alongside it.

          What the instructions do

          For a nonzero divisor and a representable signed quotient, dividend equals quotient times divisor plus remainder. A nonzero signed remainder has the dividend’s sign. Changing only the divisor’s sign changes the quotient’s sign without changing that rule.

          RISC-V gives its integer instructions defined edge results. Dividing by zero writes an all-ones quotient; the remainder is the original dividend. Dividing the most-negative signed value by -1 returns that most-negative pattern and a zero remainder. These are architectural results, not overflow flags or C error handling.

          C does not define integer division or remainder when the divisor is zero or the quotient is unrepresentable. Observe the hardware cases in this assembly exercise without treating them as valid outcomes of corresponding C expressions. Check the conditions before performing such an operation in C.

          Change it and run again

          Keep both li instructions unchanged. Replace div t2, t0, t1 with divu t2, t0, t1 and rem t3, t0, t1 with remu t3, t0, t1. Choose Build & Run and compare the t2 and t3 hexadecimal patterns in Registers with the signed version. Then restore div and rem.

          A hint

          Unsigned division sees a large positive dividend where the signed reading sees a negative one. A later reinterpretation cannot undo that instruction choice.
